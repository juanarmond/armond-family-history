# Ferreira Armond deep dig, locality catalogue, and the 1831 primary census

## Research questions

A second, deeper parallel pass (five agents) plus a primary-source read, to
turn the earlier leads into precise, Codex-ready targets and to test the
Azorean bridge harder:

1. Extract the full documented Barbacena Ferreira Armonde tree (Lacerda 2010,
   Chagas 2018) and locate any Zona da Mata / Piacatuba link.
2. Exhaustively sweep the free Leopoldina/Piacatuba parish transcriptions for
   the Simplício×Elisa marriage, Aristão's baptism and siblings.
3. Harvest the FamilySearch Research Wiki for a per-locality catalogue of
   collections/date ranges (Codex target-prep).
4. Mine ASBRAP and free RJ sources for the Muniz/Bittencourt and Azevedo lines.
5. Reconnoitre free Bohrer-family and Espírito Santo immigration resources.
6. Read the 1831 Curral Novo census (owner-supplied primary source).

## 1831 Curral Novo census — primary anti-merge confirmation

Source: "Relação dos Habitantes do Distrito de Nossa Senhora do Rosário do
Curral Novo em 1831", transcribed at
`http://www.projetocompartilhar.org/censos/1831CurralNovoMG.pdf` (11 pp; a
transcription — the manuscript original is at the Arquivo Público Mineiro).

Fogo 14 is the Ferreira Armonde household, headed by the widow **Felizarda
Maria Francisca de Assis** (70, viúva, lavradora), with her adult children all
recorded **solteiro/solteira**: **Ana Quitéria Umbelina** (40), **Simplício José
Ferreira** (46), Joaquim José (36), Antônio José (34), Manoel Ignácio (30),
Flávio José (29), Lino José (28); 66 cativos. Fogo 15 is **Manoel Ribeiro Nunes
× Carlota Teotônia da Silva** (matches the tree). The census is signed by
**"Simplício José Ferreira Armond, Juiz de Paz"** (23/09/1831).

This puts the Generation-2 Simplício on a primary document, aged 46 and single
in 1831, and as the district Juiz de Paz — he would be ~88 in 1873. He is
therefore categorically **not** the father of Marfiza (bapt. Piacatuba 1873) or
of Aristão. The anti-merge now rests on the census itself, not on a thesis
reading of it. The household is written "Ferreira" in the body and "Ferreira
Armond" in the signature — the family used the forms interchangeably.

## Thread findings

- **Barbacena tree (Lacerda 2010 doctoral thesis, document-based; Chagas 2018):**
  the full descendant tree of the immigrant Francisco Ferreira Armonde (Azores)
  was extracted. **No source places any Ferreira Armond line in
  Leopoldina/Piacatuba**; the nearest Armond blood is a female-line,
  Fonseca-surnamed branch in Além Paraíba/Aventureiro (~40 km away). So the
  Piacatuba Simplício sits in a documentary gap — the bridge is currently
  *unsupported*, not merely unproven. If a link exists, the Piacatuba Simplício
  must be a *later namesake* — most plausibly a natural/legitimised son of one
  of the several celibate Armonde brothers (Lacerda documents Marcelino José's
  four natural children, legitimised by a 1847 escritura de perfilhação at
  AHUFJF). Name-collision hazard: **Comendador Simplício José Ferreira da
  Fonseca** (different surname, same Além Paraíba region) — do not conflate.
- **Origin, sharpened:** Lacerda's primary documents (1751 testament; Mariana
  marriage book D-10) give the patriarch as **Azorean, Ilha Terceira, freguesia
  de São Sebastião** (surname from the maternal grandfather Francisco Ferreira
  Ermonde of Terceira). Only the uncited Senra blog claims a French-via-Madeira
  patriline. This is a strong documentary Azorean account vs. a weak blog claim,
  not a genuine 50/50.
- **Leopoldina/Piacatuba transcription sweep (cantoni.pro.br /
  leopoldinamg.wordpress.com — one corpus by Nilza Cantoni):** the Simplício×
  Elisa marriage is **absent** from the transcribed Leopoldina marriage index
  (1861–1930) and Conceição da Boa Vista marriages (1860–1889). The couple was
  in Piacatuba by 1873, so the decisive marriage is most likely in the
  **untranscribed Piacatuba marriage register** — the single best bridge target.
  Aristão's baptism falls just past the transcription cutoff (which ends
  mid-1879) → pull Piacatuba baptisms from 1879. Marfiza (1873) is still the only
  transcribed Armond child; no siblings appear. Lead for Elisa's origin: a
  same-parish Toledo family — Clementino José de Toledo/Tolledo × Bárbara
  Cândida de Jesus, and Francisca Emília de Toledo — navigation only.
- **Muniz/Bittencourt & Azevedo (ASBRAP, imperial-law PDFs):** no hit on João
  Monis Bittencourt, Susanna Rita Brondão, Deocleciano or Luiza (all surname
  matches are wrong-era). Refinements: Santo Antônio de Sapucaia parish was
  created 1871, dismembered from Magé; its pre-1925 books' custody is ambiguous
  — the Cúria de Niterói is ruled out (Sapucaia absent from the ASBRAP holdings
  survey), and the region is served today by the **Diocese de Valença (1925) or
  the Diocese de Petrópolis** (confirm both). The 1882 provision/habilitação is
  at the ACMRJ. The 1871 naturalisation lead is **not** in the 1871 Coleção das
  Leis (verified negative); the correct source is the **Arquivo Nacional (SIAN),
  fundo Justiça, "Cartas de Naturalização".** Bonus lead for the Armond line:
  ASBRAP Revista nº 6, "Armond, Por Quê?" (Rui Vieira da Cunha), on the Azorean
  origin of the surname.
- **Bohrer / Espírito Santo:** no free online genealogy is reachable; the origin
  question needs the primary records. Iris's 1929 birth and 1949 marriage both
  fall in Brazil, MG Civil Registration 1879–1949 (collection 3479702). In 1929
  "Presidente Soares" was only a district, so its civil registry was filed under
  the parent município — verify in the Catalog under both **Alto Jequitibá** and
  **Manhuaçu**, and disambiguate via SRC-0005 (the 1949 marriage names her
  parents João Gonçalves Bohrer and Selina/Celina Bohrer — a spelling conflict to
  preserve). Espírito Santo alternatives: ES Civil 1850–2019, ES Church
  1821–2000. The APEES "Projeto Imigrantes" DB (`imigrantes.es.gov.br`) is a
  public no-login form but self-service is postback-only; details are supplied by
  email (imigrantes@ape.es.gov.br) — an immigration lead only, never an origin
  conclusion, and do not infer German origin from the surname.

## Conclusions

- No new evidence, no promotions. The strongest result is a firmer **negative**:
  the documented Azorean Armonde tree does not reach Piacatuba, so the Azorean
  descent of Aristão is currently unsupported, and the 1831 census confirms the
  anti-merge on a primary document.
- The decisive open test is now precisely located: the **Piacatuba marriage
  register** (Simplício × Elisa), which should name Simplício's parents.
